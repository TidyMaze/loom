Loom is a minimal Android launcher that quietly learns how you use your phone.

The home screen is a calm grid of app icons, in dark or light: no notification badges, no folders, no widgets. The apps you are most likely to open are bigger, with a thin bar showing how likely. Apps you use are ordered by how likely you are to open them now, based on time of day, day of the week and the app you just closed. Apps you never open stay at the bottom, out of the way, and are one swipe away in search.

Features:
• Smart ranking computed on the device by a small scoring model.
• Swipe down anywhere to search all apps (fuzzy matching).
• Long-press an app for its shortcuts, to hide it, reset its history or open its system info.
• Pure black UI, easy on the eyes on OLED screens.
• Works without granting any runtime permission. Location, calendar and Bluetooth only refine the ranking if you allow them.

Privacy: no INTERNET permission, no account, no ads, nothing sent anywhere. If anonymous usage stats are ever added to improve the model, they will be off unless you turn them on.

On three months of the developer's own usage (10,000+ app launches), the wanted app was already first on screen about one time in three.

Early alpha. The idea comes from Yasan Launcher, thanks to its developer.
